## 摘要原文

The rapid growth of the Internet of Things (IoT) is increasing the computational demand placed on edge and backend processing infrastructures, motivating scalable architectures that can reduce execution and communication overhead. This paper presents a communication-aware comparison of homogeneous multicore classical and quantum architectures for Green IoT using the Qcomm simulator. In addition, a mesh-based, homogeneous, quantum-classical multicore architecture is proposed to enable scalable, efficient execution of distributed quantum workloads. We evaluate a 4x4 mesh of 16 processing cores under regular, classical-bottleneck, and quantum-bottleneck conditions, and study the effects of traffic pattern and Light-to-Matter (LTM) port provisioning on performance. Results show that butterfly traffic yields the largest gains over uniform traffic in both execution time and qubit coherence, while performance benefits from additional LTM ports saturate beyond four ports. At the algorithm level, homogeneous Multi Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) achieve 10x, 9x, and 7x energy efficiency improvements in comparison to Multi-CPUs for Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), and Quantum Neural Network (QNN), respectively. The paper's findings show that communication-aware multicore quantum architectures are promising for latency-sensitive IoT processing. Furthermore, the results validate the proposed architecture and provide a foundation for future scheduling and resource management techniques to improve scalability and efficiency in multi-QPU systems.
